Robot-Assisted MIS Total Knee Arthroplasty (MAKO)

Combining HEUNG-K Hospital's Minimally Invasive Expertise with Advanced Robotic Technology

MAKO Details SEE MORE SEE LESS

More Accuracy, More Precision, More Safety

Robotic-assisted knee replacement combines surgical expertise with robotic precision.

Preoperative CT scans provide a 3D view of the knee to plan implant size, placement, and bone resection. During surgery, the surgeon assesses joint balance and guides the robotic arm to precisely resect bone within the planned boundaries and position the implant.

Surgeon's Expertise+Robotic Precision

HEUNG-K Hospital's Robotic-Assisted Knee Replacement Procedure

  1. Preoperative Assessment and Planning

    A preoperative CT scan creates a 3D model of the knee to plan implant size, placement, and bone resection.

  2. Patient-Specific Surgery

    During surgery, the plan is adjusted in real time based on joint deformity and ligament condition to improve precision.

  3. Safety-Focused Surgery

    The surgeon guides the robotic arm for precise bone resection within planned boundaries. Haptic technology helps keep the cutting tool within these limits, protecting surrounding blood vessels, nerves, and soft tissues.

  4. Optimal Implant Placement

    The procedure concludes with precise placement of the selected implant in its planned position.

Umbilical Cord-derived Stem Cell (CARTISTEM®)

Systematic Treatment Tailored to Each Patient's Knee Cartilage Defect

CARTISTEM Details SEE MORE SEE LESS

CARTISTEM is a treatment for knee cartilage defects using mesenchymal stem cells derived from donated umbilical cord blood.

Stem cells are like 'seeds' that can develop into different types of cells in the body.

When implanted into damaged knee cartilage, these stem cells interact with surrounding tissues and can differentiate into cartilage cells. Through paracrine signaling, they also release growth factors and signaling molecules that may reduce inflammation and promote cartilage regeneration, supporting the formation of new cartilage.

CARTISTEM Preparation Process

  1. CARTISTEM Inspection and Preparation

    Check the condition and components of CARTISTEM before use

  2. Opening the Sterile Packaging

    Prepare for treatment under sterile conditions

  3. Stem Cell Mixing

    Mix umbilical cord blood-derived mesenchymal stem cells with hyaluronic acid

  4. Ready for Implantation

    Complete the final preparation for application to the cartilage defect

Benefits of CARTISTEM

  • Applicable to Large Cartilage Defects Without Age Restrictions

  • Potential for Effective Cartilage Regeneration After a Single Treatment

  • No Donor Site Damage, as the Patient's Own Cartilage Is Not Harvested

  • Minimally Invasive Arthroscopic Surgery Without Large Incisions

Allocostal Cartilage (MegaCarti®)

Tailored Care for Cartilage Damage: HEUNG-K Hospital's Microfracture and MEGACARTI Treatment

MEGACARTI Details SEE MORE SEE LESS

MEGACARTI is a cartilage repair material used after microfracture to protect blood clots and support cell retention and cartilage regeneration.

Microfracture creates small holes in the bone beneath damaged cartilage, allowing bone marrow stem cells and growth factors to reach the affected area. This stimulates natural healing, but the resulting fibrocartilage is less strong and durable than normal hyaline cartilage.

To address these limitations, MEGACARTI uses allogeneic costal cartilage particles containing type II collagen to protect blood clots and help bone marrow-derived cells remain at the defect site, supporting cartilage regeneration.

This treatment reinforces a weakened rotator cuff with a patch to improve repair stability and reduce the risk of re-tear.

When the tear is large or the tendon tissue is too weak for repair alone, the torn tendon is first repaired arthroscopically, then reinforced with a human dermal allograft patch or a collagen patch.

These patches support the weakened tendon, reduce stress on the repair site, and help promote tissue healing for more stable recovery. They may be considered especially in large rotator cuff tears or when severe tendon degeneration increases the risk of re-tear.

Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty (RSA)

This procedure uses the deltoid muscle to compensate for a damaged rotator cuff, helping restore shoulder function and mobility.

Reverse shoulder arthroplasty reverses the ball-and-socket arrangement of the shoulder joint, allowing the deltoid muscle to help lift the arm when the rotator cuff is damaged.

RSA is commonly considered for severe rotator cuff tears that cannot be adequately treated arthroscopically, particularly when patients cannot actively raise their arm (pseudoparalysis). By relying on the deltoid muscle, it can help restore shoulder movement and range of motion.

MICA Details SEE MORESEE LESS

MICA is a minimally invasive hallux valgus procedure that uses 2-3 mm incisions to precisely correct the deformed bone, helping reduce pain and support faster recovery.

Performed under local or regional anesthesia, MICA uses 2-3 mm skin incisions (about 3-5 small portals). Guided by real-time fluoroscopy, specialized instruments are inserted to precisely cut and realign the deformed bone.

The prominent bone is then trimmed, and the corrected bone is securely fixed with specialized screws.

Wrist & Ankle Arthroscopy

A minimally invasive procedure that uses small incisions to examine the wrist and ankle joints and precisely diagnose and treat joint damage.

Wrist & Ankle Arthroscopy Details SEE MORE SEE LESS

Wrist arthroscopy is a minimally invasive procedure that allows surgeons to examine and treat conditions inside the wrist joint.

Unlike traditional open surgery, wrist arthroscopy uses small incisions of approximately 5-10 mm to insert a camera and view joint structures in real time using high-resolution imaging.

This enables precise diagnosis and treatment of ligament, cartilage, and triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) injuries.

When Is Wrist ArthroscopyNeeded?

  • Triangular Fibrocartilage Complex (TFCC) Injury

    Damage to the tissue supporting the little-finger side of the wrist can cause pain and limited movement.

  • Wrist Ligament Tears and Instability

    Damaged wrist ligaments can cause pain and joint instability.

  • Early-Stage Wrist Arthritis

    Early cartilage wear in the wrist can cause pain, stiffness, and restricted movement.

  • Removal of Intra-Articular Loose Bodies

    Removes loose bone or cartilage fragments that cause pain or catching in the wrist joint.

  • Chronic Wrist Pain of Unknown Cause

    Helps identify and treat the cause of persistent wrist pain when other examinations are inconclusive.

A minimally invasive procedure that uses small incisions and an arthroscope to diagnose and treat conditions inside the ankle joint.

Ankle arthroscopy uses incisions smaller than 1 cm to insert an arthroscope and specialized instruments for diagnosis and treatment.

Because the ankle joint is smaller than the knee or shoulder, a 2.5 mm arthroscope designed for small joints is used for precise treatment.

Minimally Invasive Spinal Fusion

A minimally invasive spinal procedure combining nerve decompression and spinal stabilization through small incisions.

Minimally Invasive Spinal Fusion Details SEE MORESEE LESS

A minimally invasive spinal fusion procedure using an interbody cage and screws to stabilize the spine.

Through four small incisions, damaged bone and ligament tissue are removed to relieve nerve compression. The affected disc is then removed, an interbody cage is inserted for support, and the vertebrae are secured with specialized screws.

Compared with traditional open fusion, this approach uses smaller incisions and aims to reduce tissue damage. It may be considered for spondylolisthesis, severe spinal stenosis with instability, or cases where decompression alone cannot maintain spinal stability.

Benefits of Biportal Endoscopic Spinal Fusion
